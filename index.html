HOW IT WORKS

Separate the lifestyle target from the investment path.

First, Carrowmont estimates the monthly spending that would need to be funded by the portfolio. That annual amount is divided by the withdrawal-rate assumption to create an illustrative financial-independence number in today’s money.

The planner then grows that target with the inflation assumption and compares it with the projected value of current assets and future recurring investments at the selected investment frequency.

METHODOLOGY

Transparent calculations.

Portfolio-funded monthly spending: Current monthly spending × expected spending percentage − recurring non-portfolio income.

FI number today: Annual portfolio-funded spending ÷ planning withdrawal rate.

Future FI target: Today’s FI number × (1 + inflation assumption)years. Recurring non-portfolio income entered in today’s money is therefore treated as retaining the same real purchasing power.

Portfolio projection: Existing invested assets grow using the periodic rate implied by the annual return and selected investment frequency. Contributions are added once per selected investment period and can increase once each year by the entered step-up rate.

Modelled FI age: The first modelled contribution period when the projected portfolio equals or exceeds the inflation-adjusted FI target, checked through age 90.

Recurring investment required: The starting contribution at the selected investment frequency that would model to the selected-age target while applying the entered annual step-up. It is solved numerically.

Important: The withdrawal rate is only a planning assumption. The model does not determine whether a withdrawal rate is safe or suitable, and it does not model market sequence risk, taxes, fees, asset allocation, changing returns, longevity, or future policy changes unless those effects are reflected in your inputs.
